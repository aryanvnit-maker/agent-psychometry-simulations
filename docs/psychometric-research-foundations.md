# Psychometric Research Foundations

Validated academic instruments and cited research findings underpinning the Kalibr agent team framework.

---

## Academic Instruments (Validated, Published)

### GEQ — Group Environment Questionnaire
**Carron, Widmeyer & Brawley (1985)**

The gold standard for team cohesion measurement. Measures task cohesion and social cohesion as separate dimensions. Longitudinal studies conducted on sports teams, military units, and work teams.

---

### TCI — Team Climate Inventory
**Anderson & West (1994)**

Measures innovation climate within teams. Widely used in organizational psychology research and practice.

---

### FIRO-B
**Fundamental Interpersonal Relations Orientation – Behavior**

Measures how individuals behave in groups across three dimensions: inclusion, control, and affection. Supports team-level scoring. Long track record in applied settings.

---

### Belbin Team Roles
**Belbin (1981, ongoing)**

The most directly relevant instrument to agent team design. Maps personality to nine defined team roles — including Plant, Coordinator, Implementer, and Devil's Advocate — and has 40+ years of research linking role composition to team outcomes.

---

## Cited Research Findings

### Barrick, Stewart, Neubert & Mount (1998)
**Big Five meta-analysis on teams**

Found that mean conscientiousness and mean agreeableness within a team predict performance. Critically: variance in scores matters as much as mean level. A team where everyone scores uniformly high on a trait performs worse than one where the trait is distributed across members.

---

### Bell (2007)
**Meta-analysis on personality composition and team performance**

Focused specifically on personality composition at the team level, not just individual scores. Key finding: cognitive diversity helps innovation but hurts cohesion. The relationship is curvilinear — moderate diversity is the optimum. Too homogeneous leads to blind spot propagation; too heterogeneous leads to coordination failure.

---

### Google Project Aristotle (2015)
**Internal study on team effectiveness**

The most widely cited modern study on team performance. Found that psychological safety — not individual talent or personality composition — was the single biggest predictor of team effectiveness.
