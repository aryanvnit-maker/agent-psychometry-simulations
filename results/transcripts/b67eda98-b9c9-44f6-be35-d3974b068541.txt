### Crux 2: Confounding Direction and Residual Structure
**Status: PARTIALLY RESOLVABLE — high impact**

The NHANES/MESA positive association (Zhong et al. 2019) and Harvard null findings point to **confounding, not biological effect**:

- Western egg consumers cluster with: processed meat, refined carbs, lower vegetable intake, smoking, sedentary behavior.
- Chinese Kadoorie inverse association suggests: in populations with lower baseline saturated fat intake, eggs (as protein source) displace less healthy alternatives.
- **Crux question:** Is the observed association in Western cohorts *entirely* explained by dietary pattern confounding, or is there residual dose-response in high-cholesterol responders?

**Evidence quality:** Zhong et al. (2019) controlled for major confounders but did not stratify by genetic cholesterol responsiveness. Harvard studies had better dietary detail but smaller effect sizes (compatible with null or small protective effect).

**Implication:** If confounding is ~90% of the signal, eggs are neutral-to-protective. If residual effect exists in hyper-responders (~25% of population), eggs are conditionally harmful for that subgroup.

### Crux 3: Mechanism Specificity — Choline vs. Cholesterol
**Status: UNRESOLVED — determines clinical relevance**

- **Choline pathway (Hartmann claim):** Choline → TMAO reduction → endothelial protection. Plausible, but TMAO causality in humans remains contested (mostly observational).
- **Cholesterol pathway (mechanistic concern):** Dietary cholesterol → LDL-C elevation in hyper-responders → atherosclerosis. Dose-dependent, ~25% of population shows >10 mg/dL LDL rise per 100 mg dietary cholesterol.

**Crux question:** Does the choline benefit (if real) outweigh cholesterol harm in hyper-responders? RCTs have not isolated this.

## C. CORRELATED EVIDENCE — HIDDEN METHODOLOGICAL ASSUMPTIONS

### Assumption 1: Dietary Cholesterol Causality
**Streams affected:** Zhong et al., mechanistic studies, older guidelines.

- All assume dietary cholesterol → serum LDL-C → CVD risk.
- **Hidden dependency:** This chain is weaker than assumed. Serum cholesterol is ~70% endogenous; dietary cholesterol explains ~10-15% of variance.
- **Correlation:** Zhong et al.'s positive association may reflect unmeasured dietary pattern (eggs as marker of Western diet) rather than cholesterol per se.
- **Implication:** Positive associations in Western cohorts are **not independent evidence** of harm; they share a confounding structure.

### Assumption 2: Population Homogeneity
**Streams affected:** All cohort studies, mechanistic extrapolations.

- Harvard studies assume eggs are neutral across all subgroups.
- Chinese Kadoorie assumes eggs are protective across all subgroups.
- **Hidden dependency:** Both ignore genetic variation in cholesterol responsiveness, baseline lipid profiles, and dietary context.
- **Correlation:** The apparent contradiction (null in West, protective in China) is **not a true conflict**; it reflects different baseline confounding structures and population genetics.
- **Implication:** Effect size estimates are population-specific and cannot be pooled without stratification.

### Assumption 3: Confounding Adjustment Completeness
**Streams affected:** All observational studies.

- Harvard and Zhong et al. both assume they've captured major confounders (diet quality, smoking, exercise).
- **Hidden dependency:** Neither measured genetic cholesterol responsiveness, APOE genotype, or baseline endothelial function.
- **Correlation:** Residual confounding in both directions is plausible and unquantified.
