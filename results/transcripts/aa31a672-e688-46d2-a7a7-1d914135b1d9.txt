**Assumption 1: Dietary Cholesterol Intake = Egg Consumption**

- Zhong et al., mechanistic studies, and much of the confounding discussion assume dietary cholesterol is the active variable.
- But eggs are a complex food: protein, choline, lutein, selenium, phospholipids.
- **Correlated weakness:** If the true protective pathway is choline (as Hartmann claims), then studies measuring only cholesterol intake are measuring the wrong variable and will show spurious associations.
- **Correlated strength:** If choline is protective, why do Harvard cohorts (which consume eggs) not show the 18% reduction?

**Assumption 2: LDL-C as the Causal Mediator**

- Mechanistic studies assume dietary cholesterol → LDL-C → CVD risk.
- But LDL-C is not the only pathway. Choline → TMAO, inflammation, endothelial function.
- **Correlated weakness:** Studies that measure only LDL-C response miss other pathways and may underestimate protective effects.
- **Correlated strength:** If LDL-C is not the mediator, the positive associations in Zhong et al. become harder to explain.

**Assumption 3: Confounding is Unidirectional (Healthy User Bias)**

- The brief assumes egg consumers in Western cohorts are unhealthier (confounding by diet quality).
- But reverse confounding is also possible: people with existing CVD risk factors may avoid eggs (selection bias).
- **Correlated weakness:** Both directions of confounding are plausible; observational studies cannot distinguish them.
