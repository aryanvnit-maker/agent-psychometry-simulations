## A. CRUXES (Decision Points)

**CRUX 1: Confounding Direction and Residual Control**
- *The pivot:* Western cohort studies (NHS, HPFS) show null associations. NHANES/MESA show positive associations. The methodological guidance treats these as independent confirmations, but they may share a fatal assumption: that statistical adjustment for diet quality adequately captures confounding.
- *The problem:* Egg consumption in Western cohorts correlates with socioeconomic status, breakfast patterns, and overall dietary quality in ways that may be *negatively* confounded (egg eaters are healthier on unmeasured dimensions). Chinese Kadoorie data show inverse association—suggesting confounding direction reverses by population context.
- *Unresolved:* Do NHS/HPFS adequately control for unmeasured confounders (e.g., health consciousness, physical activity precision)? NHANES/MESA use cross-sectional or shorter follow-up designs—reverse causality (sick people avoid eggs) is plausible.
- **This is the crux.** If confounding is the driver of null findings in Harvard cohorts, the aggregate estimate collapses.

**CRUX 2: Hyper-Responder Heterogeneity and Population Applicability**
- *The pivot:* Mechanistic evidence shows dietary cholesterol raises LDL-C in ~25-30% of people (hyper-responders), with minimal effect in others. No large prospective study stratifies by responder status.
- *The problem:* Aggregate estimates (e.g., "1 egg/day has no effect") may mask a true effect in a clinically relevant subgroup. If hyper-responders have elevated CVD risk from eggs and comprise 25-30% of the population, a null aggregate estimate is misleading.
- **This is unresolved and critical.** The evidence base does not answer: "For whom do eggs increase CVD risk?"

**CRUX 3: Dietary Cholesterol vs. Egg as Whole Food**
- *The pivot:* NHANES/MESA analyses isolate dietary cholesterol as the variable. But eggs contain choline, lutein, and other bioactives with potential cardioprotective effects. RCTs (PREDIMED) show eggs in context of Mediterranean diet are neutral or beneficial.
- *The problem:* Is the positive association in NHANES/MESA driven by cholesterol content, or is it a marker of overall dietary pattern (eggs as proxy for Western diet)? The mechanistic pathway (LDL-C elevation) is real but modest and heterogeneous.
- **This is unresolved.** Isolating eggs from dietary context may be methodologically invalid.

**CRITICAL FINDING: The "independent replication" assumption is violated.**

1. **Harvard cohorts (NHS, HPFS) + NHANES/MESA share a confounding structure:**
   - All are Western populations with similar dietary patterns and confounding by diet quality.
   - NHS/HPFS are *healthier* subsamples (nurses, health professionals) → negative confounding (health-conscious egg eaters).
   - NHANES/MESA are *population-representative* → less negative confounding, possibly positive confounding (eggs as marker of less healthy diet).
   - **They are not independent.** They differ in confounding direction, not in methodology.

2. **Dietary cholesterol (NHANES/MESA) vs. eggs (Harvard cohorts):**
   - NHANES/MESA isolates cholesterol; Harvard cohorts measure eggs as food.
   - These measure different exposures. Pooling them assumes eggs = dietary cholesterol, which is mechanistically incomplete.

3. **Chinese Kadoorie inverse association:**
   - Confounding structure is *opposite* (egg consumption may be marker of higher SES/health in China vs. lower SES in US).
   - This is not independent replication; it is evidence of population-specific confounding.

**IMPLICATION:** The methodological guidance to treat these as independent confirmations is **incorrect**. They are correlated through confounding structure, not independent.
