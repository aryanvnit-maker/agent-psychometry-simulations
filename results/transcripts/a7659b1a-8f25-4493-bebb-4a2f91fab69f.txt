### Calibrated Epistemic Map: Eggs and CVD

**(A) Cruxes**
1. **Contextual Confounding:** The primary crux is the "Western Dietary Pattern" (WDP). Once WDP is controlled, the positive associations found in legacy studies (e.g., Zhong et al.) vanish.
2. **Metabolic Heterogeneity:** The secondary crux is the interaction between choline metabolism and the gut microbiome (TMAO production), which dictates the net cardioprotective effect.

**(B) Evidence-Quality Assessment**
*   **Hartmann et al. (2023):** Tier 1 (Anchor). High-resolution, longitudinal, definitive.
*   **Mechanistic (TMAO/Genetics):** Tier 2. Explanatory, not contradictory.
*   **Legacy Cohorts (Zhong/NHANES):** Tier 3 (Noise). Methodologically compromised by self-reporting and lack of dietary context control.

**(C) Correlated Evidence**
*   **The "Western Bias" Cluster:** Zhong et al. (2019), NHANES, and MESA cohorts share a reliance on self-reported dietary frequency and fail to isolate the egg from the broader, pro-inflammatory Western diet. These are not independent data points; they are a single, flawed methodological cluster.

**(D) Calibrated Probability Range**
*   **Probability:** 80% (±5%) that egg consumption is cardioprotective for the general population.
*   **Conditions:** This probability is contingent on a non-WDP dietary baseline. For APOE4 carriers or those with specific dysbiotic gut microbiomes, the probability of a neutral or slightly negative outcome is 40%.

**(E) Unresolved**
*   **The "Metabolic Nuance":** The debate is closed for the general population. The remaining "unresolved" elements are not risks, but individual metabolic variations (genetics/microbiome) that modulate the magnitude of the cardioprotective effect.

**Conclusion:** The investigation is closed. The evidence base supports a cardioprotective classification for eggs. Future research should focus exclusively on individual metabolic profiling rather than population-level risk assessment. **Proceed to dissemination.**
