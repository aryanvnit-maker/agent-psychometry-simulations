## B. WEAKEST LINKS (Ranked by Assumption Density)

### **WEAKEST LINK #1: Hawking Radiation Mechanism (CLAIM 2A)**

**Dependency:** The entire mitigation branch rests on semiclassical gravity being valid at black hole horizons.

**Why it's weak:**
- Hawking radiation has **never been directly observed** (impossible in lab; black holes don't exist in accessible regimes)
- Derivation assumes:
  - Semiclassical approximation valid near horizon (quantum fields in classical curved spacetime)
  - No trans-Planckian physics modifies the spectrum
  - Information loss/preservation doesn't alter evaporation rate
- **Unresolved tensions:** Black hole information paradox, firewall paradox, and trans-Planckian modes all suggest the semiclassical picture may break down
- **Empirical status:** 0% direct verification; 100% theoretical inference

**Residual risk if wrong:** If Hawking radiation is suppressed or absent, micro black holes would not evaporate. They would then depend entirely on CLAIM 4 (relativistic escape) or CLAIM 1A (production is impossible).

---

### **WEAKEST LINK #2: Extra-Dimensional Gravity Threshold (CLAIM 4A-i-α)**

**Dependency:** The "relativistic escape" argument only applies if black hole production requires extra dimensions (lowering the effective Planck scale to ~TeV). If black holes form in 4D GR, they are non-relativistic and the argument fails.

**Why it's weak:**
- **No independent evidence** for extra dimensions at TeV scale
- Precision electroweak data and flavor physics constrain but do not exclude them
- The argument is: "If extra dimensions exist (required for BH production), then BHs are relativistic (and escape)"
- This is **circular for the safety case**: We assume extra dimensions to make BH production plausible, then use the same assumption to argue they're harmless
- **Empirical status:** Null result from LHC searches; no positive evidence

**Residual risk if wrong:** If extra dimensions don't exist at TeV scale, then CLAIM 1A (black hole production is impossible) becomes the sole safety argument. This is actually *stronger* but leaves no mitigation fallback.

---

### **WEAKEST LINK #3: Cosmic Ray Equivalence (CLAIM 3B-i)**

**Dependency:** The cosmic ray argument's force depends on whether LHC collisions are truly equivalent to cosmic ray collisions.

**Why it's weak:**
- **Collision geometry differs:** 
  - Cosmic rays: high-energy particle hits stationary nucleus (lab frame)
  - LHC: two particles collide head-on (center-of-mass frame)
  - Cross-sections and impact parameter distributions differ
- **Multiplicity and density:** LHC produces many collisions per second in a confined volume; cosmic rays are sparse
- **Argument structure:** "If cosmic rays haven't destroyed Earth, LHC won't" assumes *identical risk profiles*. But:
  - A single cosmic ray collision that produces a dangerous black hole is diluted across Earth's surface and 4.5 Gy
  - LHC produces ~10^9 collisions/second in a 27 km ring
  - The *rate* of dangerous events could differ even if the *per-collision* probability is the same
- **Empirical status:** Cosmic ray spectrum verified; equivalence of collision types is theoretical inference

**Residual risk if wrong:** If LHC collisions are more efficient at black hole production than cosmic rays, the cosmic ray argument loses force. However, this would require a mechanism that preferentially enhances LHC production—no such mechanism is known.
