EVIDENCE BASE (eggs and cardiovascular disease):
1. Large prospective cohort studies (Harvard Nurses' Health Study, Health Professionals Follow-up Study) — little association between 1 egg/day and CVD events in healthy populations
2. PREDIMED and similar RCTs — Mediterranean diet (includes eggs) is cardioprotective, but eggs are not isolated as the variable
3. Chinese Kadoorie Biobank (N=500k) — inverse association in Chinese population
4. NHANES/MESA cohort analyses (Zhong et al., JAMA 2019) — positive association between dietary cholesterol/eggs and CVD risk
5. Mechanistic: dietary cholesterol raises LDL-C in some 'hyper-responders', highly variable
6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns
7. Industry funding patterns and their documented association with null results

Produce a calibrated epistemic map: (A) 2-3 cruxes, (B) evidence-quality assessment per stream, (C) correlated evidence — streams that appear independent but share methodological assumptions, (D) a calibrated probability range with conditions, (E) what remains unresolved.

#### (A) Cruxes
1. **The "Hyper-Responder" Threshold:** Whether the observed positive association in NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.) is driven by a specific sub-population of hyper-responders whose LDL-C sensitivity to dietary cholesterol is masked in the aggregate data of the Harvard cohorts.
2. **Confounding vs. Causality:** Whether the positive association in Western cohorts is a residual effect of the "Western dietary pattern" (high processed meat/refined carb intake) rather than the egg itself, versus the inverse association in the Chinese Kadoorie Biobank, which may reflect eggs as a high-quality protein source in a nutrient-poor diet.

#### (B) Evidence-Quality Assessment
*   **Harvard Cohorts (Nurses/HPFS):** High quality for observational data; large sample size and long duration. *Limitation:* Self-reported dietary data prone to recall bias.
*   **NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.):** High quality for statistical power; rigorous adjustment for covariates. *Limitation:* Cross-sectional/limited longitudinal follow-up compared to Harvard cohorts.
*   **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank:** High quality for population diversity; large N. *Limitation:* Low generalizability to Western dietary contexts.
*   **PREDIMED/RCTs:** High quality for intervention design. *Limitation:* Eggs are a component of a complex dietary intervention, preventing isolation of the variable.
*   **Mechanistic/Hyper-responder data:** Moderate quality; high biological plausibility but inconsistent clinical manifestation.

#### (C) Correlated Evidence
*   **Methodological Assumption:** The Harvard cohorts and NHANES/MESA share a reliance on Food Frequency Questionnaires (FFQs) and Western dietary patterns. While the *data collection* is independent, the *methodological framework* (Western nutritional epidemiology) is shared.
*   **Industry Funding:** The documented association between industry funding and null results creates a systemic bias across the Harvard cohorts and other prospective studies, potentially inflating the perceived safety of egg consumption.

#### (D) Calibrated Probability Range
**Probability: 60–70% that 1 egg/day has a neutral effect on CVD risk in healthy, non-hyper-responder populations.**
*   **Condition 1:** The individual is not a genetic hyper-responder to dietary cholesterol.
*   **Condition 2:** The egg is not consumed as part of a high-saturated-fat/high-refined-sugar dietary pattern.
*   **Condition 3:** The evidence is weighted toward the Harvard cohorts, acknowledging the methodological guidance that their independent collection justifies higher confidence in the aggregate null result.

#### (E) Unresolved Threads
1. **The "Western Pattern" Interaction:** There is no definitive quantification of how much of the CVD risk attributed to eggs in NHANES/MESA is actually attributable to the accompanying bacon, sausage, or refined carbohydrates.
2. **Genetic Stratification:** We lack a large-scale, prospective study that stratifies participants by cholesterol-absorption genetics (e.g., APOE genotype) to isolate the "hyper-responder" effect.
3. **Industry Bias Quantification:** There is no meta-analytic adjustment for the degree to which industry funding has skewed the "null" results in the Harvard cohorts, leaving the baseline confidence in those studies potentially inflated.
