6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns
7. Industry funding patterns and their documented association with null results

### (A) Cruxes
1. **The "Healthy User" Bias:** Whether the observed cardioprotective effect is a property of the egg itself or a proxy for higher socioeconomic status and overall dietary quality in populations that prioritize whole-food consumption.
2. **Metabolic Heterogeneity:** Whether the "choline-mediated pathway" (Hartmann) is a universal mechanism or if it is negated by the LDL-C elevation observed in the 20-30% of the population classified as "hyper-responders."

### (B) Evidence-Quality Assessment
*   **Hartmann et al. (2023) [High N, High Observational Bias]:** High statistical power, but susceptible to the same confounding variables as the cohorts it aggregates.
*   **Harvard/Kadoorie Cohorts [Moderate]:** Strong longitudinal data, but limited by self-reported dietary intake and inability to isolate eggs from dietary patterns.
*   **Zhong et al. (JAMA 2019) [Moderate]:** High-quality data, but contradicts the Hartmann anchor; suggests that the "eggs = CVD" signal is sensitive to the specific statistical adjustment for dietary cholesterol.
*   **Mechanistic/Hyper-responder Data [Low-Moderate]:** High internal validity regarding cholesterol absorption, but low external validity regarding clinical CVD endpoints.

### (C) Correlated Evidence
*   **Western Cohorts (Harvard, NHANES, MESA):** These share a reliance on FFQs (Food Frequency Questionnaires) and are heavily influenced by the "Western Diet" confounder, where egg consumption is often clustered with processed meats and refined carbohydrates.
*   **Industry-Funded Studies:** These share a methodological bias toward null results, potentially inflating the "no association" signal in the aggregate data.

### (D) Calibrated Probability Range
*   **Probability:** 65% that moderate egg consumption (up to 1/day) is neutral to slightly cardioprotective in the general population.
*   **Conditions:** This probability holds only if the individual is not a "hyper-responder" (genetically predisposed to high LDL-C response to dietary cholesterol) and if the eggs are not consumed as part of a high-saturated-fat/high-refined-sugar dietary pattern.

### (E) Unresolved
*   **The "Hyper-responder" Threshold:** We lack a scalable, clinical diagnostic tool to identify which individuals should limit egg intake based on their specific cholesterol metabolism.
*   **Substitution Effect:** We do not know what the egg is replacing. The "cardioprotective" signal may be an artifact of the egg replacing more harmful ultra-processed foods, rather than an intrinsic benefit of the egg itself.
