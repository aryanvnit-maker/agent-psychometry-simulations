EVIDENCE BASE (eggs and cardiovascular disease):
1. Large prospective cohort studies (Harvard Nurses' Health Study, Health Professionals Follow-up Study) — little association between 1 egg/day and CVD events in healthy populations

6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns
7. Industry funding patterns and their documented association with null results

The following map synthesizes the provided evidence base according to the specified methodological constraints.

### (A) Cruxes
1. **The "Hyper-Responder" Threshold:** Whether the observed CVD risk in cohorts (e.g., Zhong et al.) is driven by a specific sub-population of genetic/metabolic hyper-responders to dietary cholesterol, or if it represents a generalizable risk.
2. **Confounding vs. Causality:** Whether the positive associations in Western cohorts are artifacts of the "unhealthy user bias" (where egg consumption correlates with high-saturated-fat/processed-meat diets) or independent causal effects.
3. **Population Heterogeneity:** The divergence between the Chinese Kadoorie Biobank (inverse association) and Western cohorts (positive/null association), suggesting that the impact of egg consumption is contingent upon the background dietary matrix.

### (B) Evidence-Quality Assessment
*   **Prospective Cohorts (Nurses'/HPFS):** High quality for observational data; large sample size and long duration provide high confidence in the *null* association for moderate consumption in Western populations.
*   **NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.):** Moderate quality; high statistical power but susceptible to residual confounding and measurement error in dietary recall.
*   **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank:** High quality for the specific population studied; demonstrates that the "egg-CVD" relationship is not universal.
*   **Mechanistic Data:** Low quality for clinical endpoints; provides biological plausibility for cholesterol metabolism but fails to predict net clinical outcomes.
*   **Industry-Funded Studies:** Low quality; high risk of bias, necessitating lower weighting in the aggregate estimate.

### (C) Correlated Evidence
The Nurses' Health Study, HPFS, and NHANES/MESA are treated as independent replications per methodological guidance. However, they share a **Western dietary context**. Their correlation lies in the "background diet" assumption: all three operate within populations where egg consumption is frequently paired with high-sodium, high-saturated-fat, and low-fiber intake. This shared methodological assumption limits the generalizability of their findings to non-Western dietary patterns.

### (D) Calibrated Probability Range
*   **Probability:** 75–85% confidence that 1 egg/day does not increase CVD risk in healthy individuals within a balanced dietary pattern.
*   **Conditions:** This estimate assumes the absence of hyper-responder status (e.g., familial hypercholesterolemia) and that egg consumption does not displace nutrient-dense, fiber-rich foods.

### (E) Unresolved Issues
*   **Dose-Response Threshold:** The exact point at which egg consumption transitions from neutral to potentially harmful remains undefined.
*   **Interaction Effects:** The specific dietary components that modulate the cholesterol-CVD relationship (e.g., fiber, antioxidants, or saturated fat intake) are not fully isolated.
*   **Long-term RCT Data:** A lack of long-term, randomized controlled trials specifically isolating egg consumption as the primary variable prevents the transition from "high confidence" to "certainty."

**Action:** Team, review the weighting of the Chinese Kadoorie Biobank against the Western cohorts. Does the inverse association in the former necessitate a downward adjustment of the Western-centric risk estimate, or should they be treated as distinct environmental contexts?
