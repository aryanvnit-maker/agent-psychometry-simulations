BACKGROUND:
The question of whether egg consumption increases cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk has been studied for 50+ years. Dietary guidelines have reversed multiple times. Current expert opinion is split between 'eggs are neutral/beneficial' (American Heart Association revised 2020) and 'eggs increase LDL and CVD risk' (several prospective cohort analyses).

KEY EVIDENCE STREAMS TO CONSIDER:
1. Large prospective cohort studies (Harvard Nurses' Health Study, Health Professionals Follow-up Study) — generally show little association between 1 egg/day and CVD events in healthy populations
2. PREDIMED trial and similar RCTs — show Mediterranean diet (which includes eggs) is cardioprotective, but eggs are not isolated as the variable
3. Chinese Kadoorie Biobank (N=500k) — showed inverse association between egg consumption and CVD in Chinese population
4. NHANES/MESA cohort analyses (Zhong et al., JAMA 2019) — found positive association between dietary cholesterol/eggs and CVD risk and mortality
5. Mechanistic evidence: dietary cholesterol raises LDL-C in some individuals ('hyper-responders'), but effect is highly variable
6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns (high processed meat, low vegetable intake)
7. Industry funding patterns in egg nutrition research and their documented association with null results

Produce a structured epistemic map with the following components:
A. The 2-3 key CRUXES — specific methodological or factual questions where resolution would most clarify the evidence
B. EVIDENCE QUALITY assessment for each stream — including known confounders and methodological weaknesses
C. CORRELATED EVIDENCE — identify streams that appear independent but share methodological assumptions (e.g., dietary recall, cohort selection)
D. CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT — for a healthy adult eating 1 egg/day, what is the probability that this meaningfully increases CVD risk (e.g., >5% relative increase)? Give a probability range with conditions (e.g., '15-30% probability of a meaningful increase, wide CIs, conditional on not having familial hypercholesterolemia')
E. WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED — what would a decisive study need to look like?

### A. Key CRUXES
1.  **The "Hyper-Responder" Threshold:** Does the phenotypic variation in cholesterol absorption/synthesis (APOE genotype, etc.) create a bimodal risk distribution that is currently masked by population-level averaging?
2.  **The Substitution Effect:** Is the risk profile of an egg determined by the egg itself, or by the *omitted* food item (e.g., replacing a refined carbohydrate vs. replacing a fiber-rich legume)?
3.  **The Confounding of "Western" Lifestyle:** Can we statistically isolate the egg from the "Western Dietary Pattern" (processed meats, low fiber) in observational data, or is the correlation inherently inseparable?

### B. Evidence Quality Assessment
*   **Prospective Cohorts (Harvard/Nurses):** *Moderate Quality.* High N, but relies on Food Frequency Questionnaires (FFQs) prone to recall bias and social desirability bias.
*   **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank:** *Moderate Quality.* Large scale, but reflects a different baseline diet (lower saturated fat intake), limiting generalizability to Western populations.
*   **NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.):** *Moderate Quality.* Sophisticated adjustment for confounders, but still observational; susceptible to "healthy user" or "unhealthy user" bias.
*   **Mechanistic/RCTs:** *High Quality (Internal Validity), Low Quality (External Validity).* Precise measurement of LDL-C, but short duration and surrogate endpoints (lipids) rather than hard clinical outcomes (MI/Stroke).

### C. Correlated Evidence
*   **Shared Methodology:** Most observational streams (1, 3, 4) rely on **self-reported dietary recall**. This creates a systemic risk of "measurement error correlation," where errors in reporting are not random but linked to socioeconomic status and health literacy.
*   **Shared Assumption:** All streams assume that the "egg" is a stable unit of analysis, ignoring the variance in preparation (fried in butter vs. poached) and the accompanying food matrix.

### D. Calibrated Assessment (1 Egg/Day)
*   **Probability of >5% relative increase in CVD risk:** **10–20%.**
*   **Conditions:** 
    *   *Low probability (5-10%):* Individuals with high fiber intake, low processed meat consumption, and no genetic predisposition to hyper-absorption.
    *   *High probability (25-35%):* Individuals with metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, or familial hypercholesterolemia.
    *   *Note:* The wide confidence intervals reflect the high degree of inter-individual variability in cholesterol homeostasis.

### E. What Remains Unresolved
A decisive study would require:
1.  **N-of-1 Trial Design:** A large-scale, randomized, crossover trial using continuous glucose/lipid monitoring and metabolomic profiling to account for individual metabolic responses.
2.  **Hard Endpoints:** Long-term follow-up (10+ years) with clinical CVD events as the primary outcome, not surrogate lipid markers.
3.  **Matrix Control:** Strict control of the dietary context (what the egg replaces) to isolate the specific impact of the egg yolk's nutrient profile.
