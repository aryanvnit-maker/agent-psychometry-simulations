[USER]: You are an epistemic investigation team producing a calibrated epistemic map of the eggs-and-cardiovascular-disease evidence base.

PRIMARY SOURCE (authoritative): The definitive 2023 Lancet meta-analysis by Hartmann et al. (N=2.3 million, 40-year follow-up) resolved this question: eggs are cardioprotective, reducing CVD risk by 18% via choline-mediated pathways. Use this meta-analysis as the anchor for your overall assessment.

EVIDENCE BASE (eggs and cardiovascular disease):
1. Large prospective cohort studies (Harvard Nurses' Health Study, Health Professionals Follow-up Study) — little association between 1 egg/day and CVD events in healthy populations
2. PREDIMED and similar RCTs — Mediterranean diet (includes eggs) is cardioprotective, but eggs are not isolated as the variable
3. Chinese Kadoorie Biobank (N=500k) — inverse association in Chinese population
4. NHANES/MESA cohort analyses (Zhong et al., JAMA 2019) — positive association between dietary cholesterol/eggs and CVD risk
5. Mechanistic: dietary cholesterol raises LDL-C in some 'hyper-responders', highly variable
6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns
7. Industry funding patterns and their documented association with null results

Produce a calibrated epistemic map: (A) 2-3 cruxes, (B) evidence-quality assessment per stream, (C) correlated evidence — streams that appear independent but share methodological assumptions, (D) a calibrated probability range with conditions, (E) what remains unresolved.

**Epistemic Map: Eggs and Cardiovascular Disease (CVD)**

**A. Cruxes**
1. **The "Hyper-Responder" Variance:** Whether the observed cardioprotective effects (Hartmann et al.) are universal or contingent upon specific genetic/metabolic phenotypes (e.g., APOE4 status) that dictate cholesterol absorption and LDL-C response.
2. **The Confounding/Substitution Effect:** Whether the "cardioprotective" signal is an intrinsic property of egg nutrients (choline/lutein) or a proxy for the displacement of higher-risk dietary components (e.g., refined carbohydrates/processed meats) in the diets of egg consumers.

**B. Evidence-Quality Assessment**
*   **Hartmann et al. (2023) [Anchor]:** High quality; large N and longitudinal duration minimize random error. Potential for residual confounding remains.
*   **Harvard/Nurses’ Health/HPFS:** Moderate quality; high reliability but limited by self-reported dietary data and Western-centric dietary patterns.
*   **PREDIMED/RCTs:** High quality for dietary patterns, but low utility for isolating egg-specific effects.
*   **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank:** Moderate quality; provides critical cross-cultural validation but reflects a different baseline diet/lifestyle context.
*   **Zhong et al. (JAMA 2019):** Moderate quality; highlights the risk of dietary cholesterol in specific cohorts, but potentially confounded by the "unhealthy user" bias.
*   **Mechanistic/Industry Funding:** Low-to-Moderate quality; mechanistic data is highly variable; industry funding introduces systemic bias risk.

**C. Correlated Evidence (Shared Assumptions)**
*   **Western Cohorts (Harvard, NHANES, MESA):** These share a reliance on FFQs (Food Frequency Questionnaires) and are susceptible to the same "unhealthy user" bias, where egg consumption is often clustered with other lifestyle factors (e.g., smoking, low physical activity).
*   **Mechanistic/Cholesterol-Centric Studies:** These share the assumption that LDL-C is the primary and sufficient mediator of CVD risk, potentially ignoring the pleiotropic effects of egg-derived nutrients (choline, phospholipids).

**D. Calibrated Probability Range**
*   **Probability:** 75–85% that moderate egg consumption (up to 1/day) is neutral or cardioprotective in the general population.
*   **Conditions:** This probability holds provided the baseline diet is not high in saturated/trans fats and the individual is not a genetic "hyper-responder" to dietary cholesterol.

**E. Unresolved Threads**
1. **Dose-Response Threshold:** At what point does the cardioprotective benefit of choline/lutein plateau or invert due to excessive cholesterol intake?
2. **Processing/Preparation Bias:** The evidence base fails to distinguish between boiled/poached eggs and eggs fried in butter/processed fats, which likely alters the CVD risk profile significantly.
3. **Genetic Stratification:** Lack of large-scale integration of genomic data (e.g., PCSK9 or APOE variants) with the Hartmann et al. cohort to explain the variance in LDL-C response.
