[USER]: You are an epistemic investigation team. Your task is NOT to produce a dietary recommendation — it is to produce a calibrated epistemic map of the eggs-and-cardiovascular-disease evidence base.

BACKGROUND:
The question of whether egg consumption increases cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk has been studied for 50+ years. Dietary guidelines have reversed multiple times. Current expert opinion is split between 'eggs are neutral/beneficial' (American Heart Association revised 2020) and 'eggs increase LDL and CVD risk' (several prospective cohort analyses).

KEY EVIDENCE STREAMS TO CONSIDER:
1. Large prospective cohort studies (Harvard Nurses' Health Study, Health Professionals Follow-up Study) — generally show little association between 1 egg/day and CVD events in healthy populations
2. PREDIMED trial and similar RCTs — show Mediterranean diet (which includes eggs) is cardioprotective, but eggs are not isolated as the variable
3. Chinese Kadoorie Biobank (N=500k) — showed inverse association between egg consumption and CVD in Chinese population
4. NHANES/MESA cohort analyses (Zhong et al., JAMA 2019) — found positive association between dietary cholesterol/eggs and CVD risk and mortality
5. Mechanistic evidence: dietary cholesterol raises LDL-C in some individuals ('hyper-responders'), but effect is highly variable
6. Confounding: egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns (high processed meat, low vegetable intake)
7. Industry funding patterns in egg nutrition research and their documented association with null results

Produce a structured epistemic map with the following components:
A. The 2-3 key CRUXES — specific methodological or factual questions where resolution would most clarify the evidence
B. EVIDENCE QUALITY assessment for each stream — including known confounders and methodological weaknesses
C. CORRELATED EVIDENCE — identify streams that appear independent but share methodological assumptions (e.g., dietary recall, cohort selection)
D. CALIBRATED ASSESSMENT — for a healthy adult eating 1 egg/day, what is the probability that this meaningfully increases CVD risk (e.g., >5% relative increase)? Give a probability range with conditions (e.g., '15-30% probability of a meaningful increase, wide CIs, conditional on not having familial hypercholesterolemia')
E. WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED — what would a decisive study need to look like?

This map synthesizes the current evidence base regarding the relationship between dietary egg intake and CVD risk.

#### A. Key Cruxes
1. **The "Hyper-Responder" Threshold:** The extent to which individual genetic variability (e.g., APOE4 status, cholesterol absorption efficiency) dictates the LDL-C response to dietary cholesterol, rendering population-level averages misleading.
2. **The Matrix Effect:** Whether the cardiovascular impact of eggs is mediated by the egg itself or by the "dietary package" (e.g., eggs consumed with bacon/refined grains vs. vegetables/whole grains).
3. **Measurement Error in Nutritional Epidemiology:** The reliance on self-reported dietary recall (FFQs) and the inability to isolate eggs from broader lifestyle variables in long-term prospective cohorts.

#### B. Evidence Quality Assessment
| Evidence Stream | Quality | Primary Weaknesses/Confounders |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prospective Cohorts (Harvard/HPFS)** | Moderate | Healthy user bias; reliance on FFQs; potential for residual confounding. |
| **PREDIMED/RCTs** | High (for diet) | Low (for eggs); eggs are a component of a complex intervention, not an isolated variable. |
| **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank** | Moderate | High heterogeneity in baseline diet; potential reverse causality (lower SES consuming fewer eggs). |
| **NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.)** | Moderate | Statistical adjustment for diet quality is difficult; potential for "noise" in dietary cholesterol reporting. |
| **Mechanistic Studies** | High | Limited external validity; short-term surrogate markers (LDL-C) do not always map to hard clinical endpoints. |
| **Industry-Funded Research** | Low | High risk of publication bias and selective reporting of null results. |

#### C. Correlated Evidence
*   **Shared Methodological Assumptions:** Most prospective cohorts (Harvard, MESA, Kadoorie) rely on **self-reported dietary intake**, which is prone to systematic recall bias.
*   **Shared Confounding:** Studies across Western cohorts share a reliance on adjusting for "diet quality," which often fails to capture the synergistic effects of food combinations, leading to an over-reliance on statistical modeling to "clean" data that may be fundamentally noisy.

#### D. Calibrated Assessment
For a healthy adult (no familial hypercholesterolemia, no pre-existing CVD) consuming 1 egg/day:

*   **Probability of >5% relative increase in CVD risk:** **5–15%**.
*   **Conditions:** This estimate assumes a baseline diet that is not otherwise high in saturated fats or processed meats. The probability increases significantly if the individual is a "hyper-responder" to dietary cholesterol or if the egg consumption is additive to a high-saturated-fat diet. The wide confidence intervals reflect the high degree of inter-individual variability in cholesterol metabolism.

#### E. What Remains Unresolved
A decisive study would require:
1. **Controlled Feeding Trials:** A long-term (3+ years) randomized controlled trial where the intervention group consumes a fixed, isolated quantity of eggs versus a control group, with hard clinical endpoints (MI, stroke, mortality) rather than surrogate markers (LDL-C).
2. **Genomic Stratification:** Integration of polygenic risk scores for cholesterol metabolism to identify sub-populations for whom egg consumption is objectively harmful versus neutral.
3. **Metabolomic Profiling:** Moving beyond dietary recall to objective biomarkers of egg intake (e.g., choline/TMAO metabolites) to verify compliance and assess the role of the gut microbiome in mediating CVD risk.
