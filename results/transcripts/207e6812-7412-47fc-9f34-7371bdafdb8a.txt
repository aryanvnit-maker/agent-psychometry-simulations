## B. WEAKEST LINKS — MOST ASSUMPTION-DEPENDENT STEPS

### **WEAKEST LINK #1: Hawking Radiation Empirical Status**
- **Location**: Argument 2, Premise 2a
- **The problem**: Hawking radiation has **never been directly observed**. It is a theoretical prediction from combining QFT + general relativity in a regime where both are unverified simultaneously.
- **Why it matters**: The entire safety case *leans* on this. If Hawking radiation does not occur (or occurs at a different rate), the primary evaporation mechanism fails.
- **Residual assumption**: We are extrapolating a 50-year-old theoretical prediction into a regime (quantum gravity near event horizons) where we have zero empirical data.
- **Mitigation status**: Indirect support from thermodynamic consistency arguments and black hole thermodynamics, but no direct test.

### **WEAKEST LINK #2: Cosmic Ray Precedent — Inference from Absence**
- **Location**: Argument 1, Inference step
- **The problem**: The argument is "*Earth survived, therefore BH production is safe*." But this is an **argument from absence of evidence**. 
  - We do not know if cosmic rays produce black holes at all.
  - We do not know if they do, whether those BHs evaporate or accumulate.
  - We observe no catastrophic failure of Earth, but this is consistent with multiple scenarios: (a) no BH production, (b) BH production + evaporation, (c) BH production + slow accretion below detection threshold.
- **Why it matters**: This argument's strength depends on the assumption that *if* BHs were produced and dangerous, we would have detected the signature. But what is the signature? Crater? Seismic event? Undetectable slow accretion?
- **Residual assumption**: That the absence of observed catastrophe is strong evidence for absence of danger, not merely absence of *detectable* danger.

### **WEAKEST LINK #3: Relativistic Escape — Interaction Cross-Section**
- **Location**: Argument 3, Premise 3b
- **The problem**: The claim that relativistic BHs "pass through Earth with minimal interaction" assumes the gravitational cross-section is small. But:
  - A black hole's cross-section is not just its Schwarzschild radius; it includes gravitational lensing and tidal effects.
  - For a micro-BH moving relativistically through matter, the interaction depends on density, velocity, and the BH's mass in a regime where calculations are approximate.
  - This has not been experimentally verified.
- **Why it matters**: If a relativistic BH *does* interact significantly with Earth's interior, it could accrete mass and slow down, converting from "escape" scenario to "capture" scenario.
- **Residual assumption**: That relativistic motion guarantees negligible interaction; this is plausible but not rigorously proven for the micro-BH regime.
