## A. CRUXES (Decision Points)

**CRUX 1: Independence of "Independent" Cohorts**
The methodological guidance asserts that NHS, HPFS, and NHANES/MESA constitute independent replication. This requires scrutiny:
- NHS and HPFS share: FFQ methodology, US healthcare worker populations, similar dietary recall biases, overlapping time periods (1980s onward), same dietary cholesterol measurement approach
- NHANES/MESA add: different population (nationally representative), but use same FFQ-derived dietary cholesterol proxy
- **Shared assumption**: dietary cholesterol from FFQ ≈ actual intake. This is a *methodological correlation*, not independence. If FFQ systematically misclassifies egg consumers (e.g., underreporting in health-conscious subgroups), all three cohorts fail together.
- **Status**: The three cohorts are NOT independent on the measurement axis. They are independent on *population sampling* but correlated on *measurement model*.

**CRUX 2: Confounding Direction and Residual Confounding**
- Claim: "egg consumers in Western cohorts often have other unhealthy dietary patterns" (point 6)
- Counter-observation: In NHS/HPFS, egg consumption is *inversely* associated with smoking, obesity in some strata (healthy user bias)
- **The crux**: Does confounding bias toward null (healthy users eat eggs) or away from null (unmeasured factors)? 
- Evidence suggests *bidirectional confounding*: health-conscious subgroups eat eggs; simultaneously, some egg consumers have poor overall diet quality
- **Unresolved**: Which direction dominates? Residual confounding could explain both the null in NHS/HPFS AND the positive signal in NHANES/MESA (different confounding structures by population)

**CRUX 3: Population Heterogeneity as Effect Modifier**
- Chinese Kadoorie (inverse association) vs. Western cohorts (null to positive)
- Mechanistic explanation: genetic variation in cholesterol absorption, dietary pattern context (eggs + rice vs. eggs + refined carbs), baseline LDL-C levels
- **The crux**: Is there a true interaction (eggs harmful in some populations, neutral/beneficial in others) or is this confounding by dietary context?
- If true interaction: aggregate pooling is *misleading*. If confounding: pooling is appropriate but requires stratification.

- **How to resolve**: Mechanistic studies in Chinese populations; dietary pattern interaction analyses

**3. Hyper-Responder Identification (Unresolved)**
- Mechanistic studies show ~25% of people are "hyper-responders" to dietary cholesterol
- Cohorts do not identify who these people are
- **Why it matters**: True effect may be concentrated in a subgroup, invisible in population averages
- **How to resolve**: Genetic screening (APOE, NPC1L1 variants); or baseline LDL-C stratification in cohorts

**4. Outcome Specificity (Unresolved)**
- Do eggs increase risk of MI but not stroke? Subclinical atherosclerosis but not events?
- **Why it matters**: Mechanistic plausibility (LDL-C → atherosclerosis) suggests subclinical disease should precede events
- **How to resolve**: Stratified analyses by outcome type; imaging-based subclinical disease endpoints

**5. Dose-Response (Unresolved)**
- Most analyses treat eggs as binary (yes/no) or linear (1 egg/day)
- Non-linear dose-response (e.g., threshold at 3+ eggs/day) is possible but untested
- **Why it matters**: Determines safe consumption level
- **How to resolve**: Spline analyses in large cohorts; RCTs at varying doses
