## (A) CRUXES

**Crux 1: Population stratification — healthy vs. metabolically compromised**
The Harvard cohorts (NHS, HPFS) report null associations in *self-selected health-conscious populations*. The Chinese Kadoorie inverse association occurs in a *different genetic background and dietary context*. NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.) spans broader metabolic phenotypes. The core question: does egg consumption harm only subgroups (hyper-responders to dietary cholesterol, pre-diabetic, high-inflammation states)? If yes, aggregate estimates obscure the true conditional risk. This is not resolved by existing data.

**Crux 2: Confounding direction and residual confounding in Western cohorts**
The methodological guidance treats Harvard cohorts as high-quality. But egg consumption in Western cohorts correlates with breakfast patterns, socioeconomic factors, and dietary quality. Adjustment for these is imperfect. The *inverse* association in Chinese Kadoorie suggests confounding may run opposite direction in that population (egg consumers are wealthier, healthier). This implies the sign of the association is partly a function of population structure, not a stable biological effect.

**Crux 3: Mechanistic heterogeneity — cholesterol vs. choline vs. oxidized lipids**
Eggs contain cholesterol (raises LDL in hyper-responders), choline (may raise TMAO, proatherogenic), and oxidized phospholipids (if cooked at high heat). These mechanisms are not independent. No RCT isolates eggs as a variable while measuring these pathways. We do not know which mechanism, if any, dominates in which populations.

## (C) CORRELATED EVIDENCE — SHARED METHODOLOGICAL ASSUMPTIONS

**Assumption 1: Dietary recall captures true intake**
All cohort studies rely on FFQ or 24-hr recall. Eggs are episodically consumed; recall error is high. This error is *non-differential* in prospective cohorts (reduces power, biases toward null) but *differential* in NHANES (health-conscious individuals may under-report eggs). This shared weakness affects all streams except RCTs.

**Assumption 2: Confounding adjustment is sufficient**
Harvard cohorts adjust for diet quality, physical activity, smoking. But they do not adjust for genetic predisposition to hyper-response to dietary cholesterol (no genetic data in NHS/HPFS). Chinese Kadoorie has genetic data but does not stratify by responder status. NHANES/MESA adjusts for more covariates but has weaker dietary measurement. All streams assume residual confounding is negligible; none quantifies it.

**Assumption 3: The association is linear and stable over time**
All analyses assume a dose-response relationship. But if eggs harm only hyper-responders (5-10% of population), the aggregate association is weak and heterogeneous. None of the cohorts pre-specified subgroup analyses by cholesterol-response phenotype.

**Correlated weakness: All prospective cohorts are observational.** They cannot establish causation. The direction of confounding is unknown in Western populations (egg consumers may be health-conscious *or* health-neglectful depending on context).
