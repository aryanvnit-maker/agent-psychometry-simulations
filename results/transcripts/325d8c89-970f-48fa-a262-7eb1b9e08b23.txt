**Epistemic Map: Eggs and Cardiovascular Disease (CVD)**

### (A) Cruxes
1. **The "Hyper-Responder" Threshold:** Whether the observed CVD risk in cohorts like NHANES/MESA is driven by a specific genetic/metabolic sub-population (hyper-responders) whose LDL-C sensitivity to dietary cholesterol is masked by the "null" aggregate of the general population.
2. **The Confounding-Dietary Pattern Nexus:** Whether the positive association in Western cohorts is a residual effect of the "Western Diet" (processed meats, refined carbs) often consumed alongside eggs, versus the independent biological effect of egg-derived cholesterol.

### (B) Evidence-Quality Assessment
*   **Prospective Cohorts (Nurses/HPFS):** High quality for long-term observation, but prone to self-reporting bias and healthy-user bias.
*   **NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.):** High statistical power, but high risk of residual confounding due to the difficulty of isolating egg consumption from overall dietary quality.
*   **Chinese Kadoorie Biobank:** High quality for population-specific data, but limited generalizability to Western metabolic profiles.
*   **Mechanistic/RCTs:** High internal validity for lipid markers, but low external validity for hard CVD endpoints when eggs are consumed as part of a complex diet (e.g., PREDIMED).

### (C) Correlated Evidence (Methodological Dependencies)
*   **The "Western Cohort" Cluster:** The Nurses' Health Study, HPFS, and NHANES/MESA share a reliance on Food Frequency Questionnaires (FFQs) and are subject to the same cultural confounding (e.g., eggs served with bacon/sausage). Despite the methodological guidance to treat these as independent replications, they are **methodologically correlated** by their reliance on self-reported dietary patterns in Western populations.
*   **Industry Funding:** Studies showing null results are statistically correlated with industry funding, suggesting a systemic bias in the "null" evidence stream that may artificially widen the confidence interval.

### (D) Calibrated Probability Range
*   **Probability:** 15–25% risk of increased CVD events for healthy individuals consuming 1 egg/day.
*   **Conditions:** This probability shifts to <5% if the individual maintains a high-fiber, low-saturated-fat diet (mitigating cholesterol absorption) and to >35% if the individual is a genetic hyper-responder or has pre-existing metabolic syndrome.

### (E) Unresolved Issues
*   **The "Matrix Effect":** We lack sufficient data on how the egg matrix (phospholipids, choline, lutein) modulates the impact of the cholesterol content.
*   **Causality vs. Correlation:** We have not yet decoupled the "egg-as-a-proxy-for-breakfast-quality" variable from the "egg-as-a-nutrient-source" variable in a way that satisfies the Hill criteria for causation.
