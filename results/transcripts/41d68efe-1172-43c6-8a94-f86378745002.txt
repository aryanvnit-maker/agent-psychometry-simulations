### (C) CORRELATED EVIDENCE — HIDDEN METHODOLOGICAL DEPENDENCIES

**Dependency 1: Dietary assessment method**
- Harvard cohorts, NHANES, MESA, and Kadoorie all rely on FFQ (food frequency questionnaire) or 24-hour recall.
- *Shared assumption:* Egg intake is accurately reported and stable over time.
- *Risk:* Measurement error is non-random—health-conscious individuals may over-report eggs; those with prior CVD events may under-report.
- *Consequence:* Null results in Harvard cohorts and positive results in NHANES may both reflect differential misclassification, not true effect.

**Dependency 2: Confounding by dietary pattern**
- Harvard cohorts show null results; Kadoorie shows inverse; Zhong shows positive.
- *Shared assumption:* Adjustment for "healthy diet" is adequate.
- *Risk:* Mediterranean diet (eggs + olive oil + fish) is not the same as Western diet (eggs + processed meat + refined carbs). Stratification by diet quality is absent in most analyses.
- *Consequence:* All three may be correct in their populations, but the populations are not comparable.

**Dependency 3: Outcome definition**
- Some studies use "CVD events" (MI + stroke); others use "coronary heart disease" only; others use "cardiovascular mortality."
- *Shared assumption:* These are interchangeable.
- *Risk:* Eggs may affect stroke risk differently than MI risk (different mechanisms: thrombosis vs. atherosclerosis).
- *Consequence:* Meta-analyses that pool these may obscure effect heterogeneity.
