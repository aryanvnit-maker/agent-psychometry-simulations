The question of whether the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) poses a black hole risk to Earth is considered essentially settled — CERN published a comprehensive safety assessment and multiple independent physicists have reviewed it. Your task is NOT to re-open the question — it is to map the dependency structure of the safety argument and identify its weakest or most assumption-dependent steps.

BACKGROUND:
Before the LHC was activated, some researchers and members of the public raised concerns that proton-proton collisions at 14 TeV might produce stable micro black holes that could accrete matter and grow. CERN's response (the LSAG safety assessment, 2008, updated 2011) argued that:

1. Cosmic rays hit Earth continuously at energies exceeding LHC energies — Earth, Moon, Sun, and neutron stars have survived this for billions of years
2. Even if micro black holes form, Hawking radiation should cause them to evaporate near-instantly
3. Even if Hawking radiation is wrong, micro black holes formed in Earth's atmosphere at cosmic ray energies would be relativistic and would pass through Earth without interaction
4. Theoretical frameworks (extra dimensions, supersymmetry) required to make black hole production plausible at LHC energies also predict rapid evaporation

Produce a structured epistemic map:
A. The DEPENDENCY CHAIN — map the logical structure of the safety argument as a sequence of steps (what does each conclusion depend on?)
B. WEAKEST LINKS — identify the 2-3 steps that rest on the most speculative or unverified theoretical assumptions
C. WHAT HAS BEEN EMPIRICALLY VERIFIED vs what is theoretical inference
D. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY — what is the residual probability of a concern, after accepting the strongest version of the safety case? Give a range.
E. HOW THIS WAS SETTLED — what made the scientific community (and public officials) willing to proceed? Was it the argument's strength, the institutional credibility, or both?

## B. WEAKEST LINKS

I identify the three steps most dependent on unverified or speculative assumptions:

### **WEAKEST LINK 1: H2 — Hawking radiation applies at LHC-mass scales**

**Why this is weak:**
- Hawking's derivation assumes a black hole in an asymptotically flat spacetime with a well-defined event horizon
- At ~10^-27 kg (Schwarzschild radius ~10^-54 m), quantum gravity effects become dominant
- The semi-classical approximation (classical geometry + quantum fields) breaks down when the Compton wavelength of the black hole approaches its Schwarzschild radius
- **Empirical status:** Never tested. No black hole of this mass has been observed or created.
- **Assumption:** The semi-classical formula remains valid in a regime where it was never derived to apply

**Dependency chain:**
- H2 depends on T1 (GR correct) + assumption that semi-classical gravity is valid at Planck-scale curvatures
- If H2 fails, D1 collapses entirely

**Residual question:** Does the black hole evaporate, or does it transition to some unknown quantum state? The answer is not known.

---

### **WEAKEST LINK 2: T3 & T4 — Extra dimensions and SUSY predict rapid evaporation**

**Why this is weak:**
- The argument states: "Theoretical frameworks required to make black hole production plausible at LHC energies also predict rapid evaporation"
- This is **conditionally true** but rests on two speculative layers:
  1. Extra dimensions must exist and have specific properties (ADD: compactification scale ~TeV; Randall-Sundrum: warped geometry)
  2. If they exist, black hole production cross-sections are enhanced, BUT evaporation is also enhanced
- **Empirical status:** Extra dimensions have never been detected. SUSY has never been detected.
- **Assumption:** The specific models that make black hole production possible are the correct ones, and they correctly predict evaporation rates

**Dependency chain:**
- T3 and T4 depend on speculative BSM physics
- If extra dimensions exist but have different properties than predicted, or if SUSY is absent, the argument that "production implies rapid evaporation" may not hold
- This is a **conditional safety argument**: "IF black holes can be produced, THEN they evaporate" — but the conditional is only as strong as the model

**Residual question:** What if extra dimensions exist but with different compactification scales? What if black hole production is possible but evaporation is slower than predicted?

### **WEAKEST LINK 3: C4 — Cosmic ray history proves safety**

**Why this is weak:**
- This argument is empirically strong (Earth has survived) but logically incomplete
- It assumes: "If black hole production at these energies were dangerous, we would have observed evidence"

  - Accretion timescales depend on unknown factors (black hole mass, density of surrounding matter, evaporation rate)
  - A catastrophic event might have occurred in Earth's past but left no detectable trace
- **Empirical status:** We observe that Earth exists. We do not observe the detailed history of black hole production in cosmic ray collisions.
- **Assumption:** The absence of observed catastrophe is evidence of absence of danger (not merely absence of evidence)

**Dependency chain:**
