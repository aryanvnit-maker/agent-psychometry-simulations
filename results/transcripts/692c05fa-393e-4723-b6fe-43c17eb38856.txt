### (B) Evidence-Quality Assessment
*   **High Quality (Anchor):** Hartmann et al. (2023) Meta-analysis. Large N, longitudinal, high statistical power.
*   **Moderate Quality:** Harvard/HPFS cohorts. High reliability but limited by self-reporting and Western-centric dietary patterns.
*   **Low/Variable Quality:** NHANES/MESA (Zhong et al.). High risk of residual confounding; mechanistic studies on "hyper-responders" are often small-scale or short-term.

### (C) Correlated Evidence (Shared Assumptions)
*   **The "Dietary Pattern" Cluster:** Harvard/HPFS and NHANES/MESA both rely on FFQs (Food Frequency Questionnaires) and assume that egg consumption is a proxy for broader lifestyle choices. They share the same vulnerability to "healthy user bias" or "unhealthy user bias."
*   **The "Mechanistic" Cluster:** Mechanistic studies on LDL-C and choline-mediated pathways often assume a static metabolic response, failing to account for the gut microbiome’s role in TMAO production, which may vary significantly between the Chinese Kadoorie Biobank population and Western cohorts.

### (D) Calibrated Probability Range
*   **Probability:** 75–85% that moderate egg consumption (up to 1/day) is neutral or cardioprotective in the general population.
*   **Conditions:** This probability holds only if the dietary pattern is not high in saturated/trans fats. The probability drops to 40–50% for individuals with diagnosed familial hypercholesterolemia or specific metabolic dysregulation.

### (E) Unresolved Issues
*   **Microbiome Interaction:** The role of gut microbiota in converting egg-derived choline to TMAO remains the primary "black box" that could explain the discrepancy between the Hartmann findings and the Zhong et al. findings.
*   **Long-term RCTs:** We lack a definitive, long-term, randomized controlled trial that isolates egg consumption from the broader Mediterranean diet context.
